# Business model

**Status:** hypotheses to test, not facts. Numbers below are placeholders to replace with data from
the tests described. No figure here is a market study.

## Who benefits financially from well-informed newcomers

| Beneficiary | Gain | Likely payment |
|---|---|---|
| **Employers** (tech companies, hospitals, universities) | Lower early churn, faster onboarding, fewer HR questions | B2B licence per employee-seat or per community channel |
| **Tourism companies** (resorts, tour operators) | Seasonal staff arrive informed and stay the season | Seasonal licence, per-season group channel |
| **Universities and universities of applied sciences** | Better experience for international students and researchers | Annual institutional licence |
| **Municipalities and services** (city, healthcare districts) | Reach residents they cannot reach in Finnish; fewer misunderstandings | Service contract for official-information channels, grants |
| **Foundations and public funders** | Language rights, integration, media diversity | Grants, especially for Sámi and Swedish content |
| **Kaleva Media** | New audience, new inventory, a new product line | Internal investment, shared revenue |

## Willingness to pay (and how we would test it)

* **Tech professionals and researchers**: can pay, but the employer is the likelier payer.
  Test: three paid pilots with employers; ask for a price in discovery calls before building more.
* **Seasonal workers**: low income and a short stay, so individuals will probably not pay.
  The employer pays for the channel; the worker gets it free. Test: pilot with one resort for one season.
* **Students**: price sensitive. Test: institution licence versus a student price.
* **Residents with refugee background**: should not be asked to pay. Funding through municipalities and foundations.
* **Sámi and Swedish speakers**: public-interest funding (language-protection grants, Sámi Parliament
  programmes, media support), possibly Kaleva Media's own cross-subsidy.

Tests: a landing-page pre-sign-up with price tiers, a paid pilot with a letter of intent, retention and
"would you be disappointed if this disappeared" surveys after four weeks.

## Revenue streams (combination)

1. **B2B licences** (employers, tourism companies, institutions): main stream.
2. **Municipal and public contracts** (official-information channels).
3. **Grants and foundations** (minority languages, integration).
4. **Recruitment and housing listings**, clearly labelled as paid, outside editorial content.
5. **Freemium for individuals**: free news and onboarding pack; paid extras (priority questions, digests).
6. **Events**: onboarding days with employers and universities.

## Independence: when the payer is the subject

Rules written down before any contract is signed:

* Funders and licensees **never** pick stories, see drafts early, or get changes after publication.
* A funding relationship that touches a story's subject is **disclosed on the story**.
* Editorial decisions sit with the editor-in-chief; the human gate and the audit trail make this visible.
* Contracts say the medium may report critically on its payers. Contracts that forbid it are declined.
* Advertising and recruitment content is labelled and kept apart from editorial content.
* The community board (trusted voices) can review how funding was disclosed.

## Link to Kaleva Media

* **New subscribers**: employers and institutions buy channels; Kaleva's local reporting is the source
  that makes the product credible.
* **Advertising inventory**: new audiences in new languages for local advertisers (clearly labelled).
* **New revenue**: licences and public contracts that did not exist before.
* **Reuse of reporting**: Kaleva stories are paywalled. Licensed reuse of Kaleva reporting for
  translation and explanation is a natural contribution and creates a reason to subscribe to the original.

## Cost per community, and break-even

Cost drivers per language and community: model usage (translation, explanation, checks), human
review time (editor, language reviewer, subject expert), channel costs (messaging, audio), and hosting.
The audit log records token use and review minutes per story, so this can be measured, not guessed.

Break-even (to be filled with measured costs): `monthly cost = stories/month × (model cost + review
minutes × reviewer rate) + fixed hosting`, versus `licences × price`. Smaller languages cost more per
story in human time and are funded by grants, not by volume.

## Risks

* Employers may expect favourable coverage: independence rules above and the right to decline.
* Review time is the real cost: keep stories few, useful and high quality.
* Overpromising on languages: add a language only when reviewers exist.
