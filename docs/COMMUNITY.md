# The community runs this with us

## Who we start with, and why

| Community | Why first | Language | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **International newcomers in Oulu** (tech workers, students, researchers, families) | Large, growing, urgent practical needs (permits, housing, health, transport), employers and universities have a reason to pay | English, then Ukrainian and Arabic | Group message, weekly digest, web |
| **Seasonal tourism workers in Lapland** | The clearest case of the short life cycle; information is needed in the first week; a recurring set of payers (resorts, tour operators) | English, then Russian | Messaging group, short audio |
| **North Sámi speakers** (language pilot) | There is almost no local news in Sámi, and wrong machine output harms the language, so it shows how we protect small languages | North Sámi (human only), Finnish | Messaging, audio |

Scaling rule: a new community is a config entry (language lanes, trusted voices, channels, lifecycle),
not new code. Smaller towns and less common languages work the same way: fewer reviewers, more
`human_only` lanes, slower cadence.

## How the community takes part (not just as an audience)

* **Trusted voices** are the community's own **admins and moderators**: the people who already run it,
  such as a student buddy, a migration adviser, a resort HR officer, a union representative or a Sámi
  language teacher. Each is appointed for a community and given specific review roles and languages.
  They review in their language and domain, and their name is shown beside what they approved.
  Community roles in the review gate can only be filled by these people.
* **Questions**: answered only from published stories and maintained cultural context, otherwise a
  human is asked. The newsroom never invents an answer.
* **Tips and local knowledge**: become leads for the news editor. A tip is never a fact. Local knowledge
  becomes a context note attributed to the community after a human accepts it.
* **Corrections and flags**: anyone can ask for a correction. It opens a `Correction`; a named editor
  publishes it and says what was wrong.
* **Deciding what matters**: the news editor weights the user needs (information, understanding,
  usefulness, emotion, break, community) per community from `config/communities.yaml`. Trusted voices
  propose changes to those weights. A quarterly review checks the weights against what members read
  and ask for.
* **Holding the medium to account**: the audit trail (every agent decision and reason) can be shown to
  community reviewers. Disagreements and corrections are public.

## Why trust this over a Facebook group

* Every story links its original sources, says it was made with AI, and names the approving human.
* Mistakes are corrected publicly, and the correction says what was wrong.
* Official sources come first for permits, health and work.
* Syndicated copies are not counted as confirmation; disputes are shown, not hidden.
* The people who know the community review in its language.

## The short life cycle

Seasonal communities have a `Lifecycle` (typical stay, onboarding window). The distributor builds a
first-week pack of the most useful recent items for a new arrival, so people get what they need on
arrival and not months later. Content that stops being relevant is not pushed again.

## Stories about the communities, for Finnish speakers

`bridge_digest` creates a short Finnish digest of approved stories about newcomers and minorities,
so local residents also see what is happening to their neighbours.

## Small languages

Machine translation for the Sámi languages is weak and mistakes can offend. So tier C means: the
machine never produces publishable text. It can supply glossary hints to a human translator who
writes the text, and a Sámi language reviewer approves it. The community, not the newsroom, sets
naming and terminology.
